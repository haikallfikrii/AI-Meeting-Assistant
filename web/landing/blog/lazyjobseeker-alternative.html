LazyJobSeeker Alternative for Interviews and Client Calls

Job-search copilots have a built-in expiry date: the moment you accept an offer, the product stops having a job. Kalfi is built for the same interview moment, then keeps working for client calls, sales conversations and internal meetings. Here is how the two compare.

14 September 2026 · 6 min read · Kalfi team

The short version

  • LazyJobSeeker is organised around a job hunt; Kalfi is organised around live calls of any kind.
  • Kalfi ships three modes: interview, client meeting and general chat, each with its own context form.
  • Project threads carry context across meetings, which matters far more for client work than for a one-off interview.
  • $14/month BYOK or $19/month hosted, with no free session to run out of.
  • Kalfi does not write resumes or auto-apply. If that is your main need, it is the wrong tool.

The expiry problem

A job search takes somewhere between a few weeks and a few months. Tools built for that window optimise for it correctly: application tracking, resume tailoring, a free first session to prove value, then a paid plan sized for a short burst of intense use.

The problem shows up afterwards. You have learned that an overlay whispering a good first sentence is genuinely useful, and now you are in a client kickoff or a renewal call where the same help would be worth more than it was in the interview. The job-search product has nothing to say about a project scope, a delivery date, or what you promised on the last call.

Three modes, three context forms

Kalfi's structure is a direct response to that. Each mode asks only for what it needs, rather than forcing meeting work through an interview-shaped form.

  • Interview mode. Job description plus your resume. Answers are calibrated to the role and to the experience you actually have on paper.
  • Client meeting mode. Project scope and open decisions. Answers stay inside what has been agreed, which is what stops a copilot from cheerfully promising a deadline you cannot hit.
  • General chat mode. Everything else: internal syncs, vendor calls, or language support on a call in your second language.

Threads are the difference for client work

Interviews are mostly isolated events. Client relationships are not. Kalfi groups meetings into a project thread, generates a per-meeting summary, and lets the next session start with the accumulated context: scope changes, open questions, commitments made.

Practically, this means the second call with a client does not begin with you re-explaining the project to your own tool. You can browse earlier meetings without disturbing the live session, which matters when someone asks what was decided about the invoicing flow in the middle of a call.

What it looks like during a call

Kalfi runs as a macOS Electron app with no Dock or Cmd+Tab icon, drawing a panel window above fullscreen Google Meet or Zoom. It listens to system audio, your microphone, or both mixed, which covers the common case where you repeat a question back to the room before answering it.

When it catches a question, it writes one speakable line first and supporting points underneath. If detection misses something, Ask and Mic Ask force an answer on the last line. You set length and tone before the session, so a formal client review and a casual internal sync do not get the same voice.

The overlay window is content-protected, so it should not appear when you share your screen, and you can rename the app and replace its logo so nothing on your machine is labelled as an AI copilot. The stealth article explains exactly how far that goes.

The honest caveats. Kalfi is not a rootkit and is not undetectable. Screen-capture protection depends on your macOS version and the conferencing app, so test your own setup before a call that matters. And follow the rules of the room: if an employer, school or client prohibits assistance, that settles it.

Who should not switch

  • You want resume rewriting, cover letters or application automation. Kalfi does none of that.
  • You are on Windows or Linux. Build targets exist, but macOS on Apple Silicon is what ships tested.
  • You have two interviews left and then you are done. A short-term plan elsewhere may cost less in absolute terms.

Otherwise, setup takes about ten minutes: grab the DMG from GitHub Releases, choose BYOK or hosted, set your display name, and run one test call before a real one.

Questions

Can I really use this on client calls?

That is the mode it was designed around. Client meeting mode takes project scope and open decisions, and sessions sit in threads so follow-up calls inherit the history and the commitments.

Does Kalfi write my resume or apply to jobs?

No. It is a live-call copilot and nothing else. If application tooling is the main thing you are paying for, keep a dedicated job-search product.

Is there a free session?

No. Kalfi is $14/month on your own key or $19/month hosted, with no expiring trial and no per-session metering. On BYOK you also pay your provider for what you use.

Does anyone in the meeting see a bot?

No. Audio is captured locally on your Mac and the overlay is drawn on your own display. No extra participant appears in the roster.

What about a team of consultants?
